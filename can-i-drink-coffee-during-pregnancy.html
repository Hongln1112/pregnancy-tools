Short answer
Yes — up to about 200 mg caffeine a day.

That's roughly one 8-oz (240 ml) cup of brewed coffee. Keep tea, cola, chocolate, and energy drinks in mind too — they all count toward that daily total.

How much caffeine is in common drinks?

Your "safe" total is around 200 mg per day. This table helps you see where your cup sits:

DrinkTypical caffeine
Brewed coffee (8 oz / 240 ml)~95 mg
Instant coffee (8 oz)~60 mg
Espresso (1 shot, 1 oz)~63 mg
Black tea (8 oz)~47 mg
Green tea (8 oz)~28 mg
Cola (12 oz / 355 ml)~30–40 mg

Why the limit exists

Caffeine passes through the placenta to your baby, who cannot break it down as quickly as you can. In higher amounts, it can raise heart rate, affect sleep, and some studies link heavy intake with a slightly higher risk of miscarriage, restricted growth, or lower birth weight.

The sweet spot: one regular cup in the morning is usually fine. It's the second, third, or "extra-strong" servings through the day that push you over the limit.

Practical tips

  • Have your cup in the morning — caffeine late in the day often worsens pregnancy insomnia.
  • Swap one coffee for decaf or herbal tea in the afternoon.
  • Remember tea, cola, chocolate, and energy drinks add to the same daily total.
  • If you feel jittery, your heart racing, or you're not sleeping well, cut back further.
  • Stay hydrated — water counts, and it helps flush caffeine.

Good low-caffeine alternatives

  • Herbal teas (ginger, peppermint, chamomile — check they're pregnancy-safe)
  • Decaf coffee (small amount of caffeine, far less)
  • Warm milk, oat milk, or fruit-infused water

Common questions

Can I drink coffee while pregnant?▾
Yes. Most health authorities including ACOG agree that up to about 200 mg of caffeine a day is safe during pregnancy. That's roughly one 8-oz (240 ml) cup of brewed coffee.
How much caffeine is too much during pregnancy?▾
More than 200 mg a day is where caution starts. Some studies link higher intakes with a slightly increased risk of miscarriage, low birth weight, or restricted growth.
Does caffeine cross the placenta?▾
Yes. Caffeine passes through the placenta to your baby, whose body breaks it down much more slowly than yours.
What about tea, cola, and energy drinks?▾
They all count toward your daily caffeine total. Tea, cola, chocolate, and energy drinks add up — keep the combined total under 200 mg.
Is decaf caffeine-free?▾
No. Decaf coffee still contains a small amount of caffeine, usually around 2-15 mg per cup. It's a good lower-caffeine choice but not strictly zero.
