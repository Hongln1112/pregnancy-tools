Morning sickness (nausea) First trimester

Nausea and occasional vomiting are very common, usually peaking in the first trimester and easing around 12–14 weeks. It can happen any time of day, not just the morning.

Helpful tips

  • Eat small, frequent meals to avoid an empty stomach.
  • Keep plain crackers or ginger by the bed for before you get up.
  • Try ginger tea or foods, and avoid strong smells.
  • Ask your care team about vitamin B6 — only with their advice.

When to seek help

  • Vomiting so severe you can't keep fluids down.
  • Losing weight or signs of dehydration.
  • Dark urine, dizziness or weakness.

Back pain Any trimester

Your growing baby shifts your centre of gravity, and relaxing pregnancy hormones loosen joints — together these often cause lower back discomfort, especially later on.

Helpful tips

  • Stand and sit with a straight, supported posture.
  • Sleep on your side with a pillow between your knees.
  • Use a maternity support belt and supportive shoes.
  • Gentle walking and pregnancy-safe stretching help.

When to seek help

  • Sudden, severe back pain, especially with a fever.
  • Pain that doesn't ease or wakes you at night.
  • Back pain along with burning when you pee.

Swelling (edema) Third trimester

Mild puffiness in the feet and ankles is normal in late pregnancy as extra fluid builds up. It tends to worsen in warm weather or after long standing.

Helpful tips

  • Rest with your feet raised above heart level.
  • Avoid sitting or standing still for too long.
  • Drink enough water and keep salt intake moderate.
  • Wear comfortable, non-restrictive shoes.

When to seek help

  • Sudden or severe swelling, especially in the face or hands.
  • Swelling that's mainly in one leg.
  • Swelling along with a severe headache or vision changes.

Heartburn Second–third trimester

Pregnancy hormones relax the muscle between your stomach and food pipe, and your growing uterus pushes upward — a common recipe for heartburn as pregnancy advances.

Helpful tips

  • Eat smaller meals and avoid eating right before lying down.
  • Skip spicy, greasy or very acidic foods if they bother you.
  • Stay upright for a while after meals.
  • Sleep with your head slightly raised.

When to seek help

  • Pain that feels severe or doesn't respond to diet changes.
  • Difficulty swallowing or persistent chest pain.
  • Ask your care team before taking any antacid.

Fatigue First & third trimester

Feeling unusually tired is normal, especially early on while your body adjusts, and again in the third trimester as carrying your baby takes more energy.

Helpful tips

  • Go to bed a little earlier and nap when you can.
  • Keep a gentle routine of daily movement.
  • Stay hydrated and eat regular, balanced meals.
  • Check with your care team about iron levels.

When to seek help

  • Fatigue that's severe or constant despite rest.
  • Pale skin, breathlessness or dizziness.
  • Always-tired feelings could relate to anaemia.

See your care team right away if you have

  • Heavy or painful bleeding from the vagina.
  • Sudden severe headache, blurred vision or seeing spots.
  • Severe upper abdominal or shoulder pain.
  • Sudden, severe swelling or puffiness of the face and hands.
  • Signs of infection such as a high fever or painful urination.

Common questions

Is morning sickness normal?▾
Yes. Nausea affects many pregnant women, most often in the first trimester, and usually eases by 12–14 weeks. Severe vomiting that prevents eating or drinking needs medical attention.
What pregnancy symptoms are normal vs warning signs?▾
Nausea, fatigue, breast tenderness, mild swelling and backache are common. Sudden severe headache, vision changes, upper abdominal pain, heavy bleeding or sudden heavy swelling can be warning signs — contact your care team.
When should I worry about swelling?▾
Mild swelling in the ankles is common. Worry if it's sudden, severe, only in one leg, or comes with a severe headache or vision changes — that can signal pre-eclampsia or a clot.
