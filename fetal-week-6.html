Week 6 of pregnancy

Week 6 — The heartbeat begins

This week your baby is about the size of a lentil.

What's happening this week

  • The heart starts to beat — a tiny heartbeat that can often be detected on an ultrasound.
  • The first arm and leg buds begin to appear.
  • The neural tube, which becomes the brain and spinal cord, is forming.
Source: Mayo Clinic, Prenatal care in your first trimester. Learn more

Questions about week 6

I'm feeling nauseous all the time — is this normal?▾
Yes, morning sickness is one of the most common early pregnancy symptoms. Despite the name, it can hit at any time of day. Small, frequent meals, ginger crackers, and staying hydrated can help. If you can't keep anything down for more than 24 hours or you're losing weight, call your doctor — you might have hyperemesis gravidarum.
When will I hear the heartbeat?▾
Your baby's heart started beating around week 6, but you probably won't hear it on a Doppler until around 10–12 weeks. On an ultrasound, you can usually see the heartbeat flicker as early as 6–7 weeks. Seeing that heartbeat is a huge relief for most parents — it's the first real sign your pregnancy is progressing well.
Can I tell people I'm pregnant now?▾
That's totally up to you. Many people wait until after the first trimester (around 12 weeks) to share the news, because the risk of miscarriage drops significantly. But if you're excited and want to tell family and close friends now, that's perfectly fine too.
What vitamins should I be taking?▾
You should be taking a prenatal vitamin with at least 400–800 mcg of folic acid every day — ideally before you even got pregnant. Folic acid helps prevent neural tube defects. Ask your doctor which brand they recommend, and whether you need extra vitamin D, iron, or DHA.
