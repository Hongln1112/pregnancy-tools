Why twins usually arrive earlier

Twins due date ≈ first day of last period + 259 days (37 weeks)

Carrying two babies puts more demand on your body and the placenta, so twin pregnancies are usually delivered between 34 and 37 weeks to keep everyone safe. Most care teams plan for around 37 weeks — that's why this calculator uses 37 weeks instead of the singleton 40-week rule.

Exactly when your twins arrive depends on your health and whether they share a placenta. Your doctor or midwife will guide the timing with regular scans; treat this estimate as a planning tool, not a fixed date.

Typical twin delivery windows

Type of twin pregnancyTypical delivery window
Dichorionic twins (two placentas)Usually 37–38 weeks
Monochorionic twins (shared placenta)Usually 34–37 weeks, planned earlier
Monoamniotic twins (shared sac)Usually 32–34 weeks, under close monitoring

These are general ranges, not rules. Your individual plan depends on growth scans, your health, and how your pregnancy is progressing.

Common questions

Why is a twins due date earlier than a singleton due date?▾
Twin pregnancies usually deliver between 34 and 37 weeks. Most care teams plan for around 37 weeks because carrying twins past that point raises the risk of complications. This calculator uses 37 weeks (259 days) as its estimate.
Is 37 weeks considered full term for twins?▾
Yes. For twins, 37 weeks is generally considered full term. For comparison, a singleton pregnancy is full term from 39 weeks. Your care team may plan earlier if there are shared blood flow or growth concerns.
Can a twins due date calculator be exact?▾
No calculator can be exact for twins. The actual delivery time depends on your health, whether your twins share a placenta, and how your pregnancy progresses. Treat this as a planning estimate and follow your care team's guidance.
Do twins usually need a planned C-section?▾
Not always. Many twins can be born vaginally if the first baby is head-down and both are healthy. A planned C-section is common for certain positions or shared-placenta twins. Talk to your care team about your individual plan.
