How to use this converter

  • Pick the date you know best: the first day of your last period, your ovulation date, or your conception date.
  • Enter that date and tap Calculate my week — you'll see your week and day, plus which trimester you're in.
  • Pregnancy weeks are always counted from the last period, so ovulation and conception dates are automatically shifted back two weeks.

How the three dates relate

Last period → ovulation & conception happen about 2 weeks later

With a typical 28-day cycle, you ovulate around day 14 of your cycle, and conception happens around that same time — about two weeks after the first day of your last period. Because pregnancy is dated from the last period, a conception date of, say, June 14 aligns with week 2, and your last period would have been around May 31.

Trimesters: First trimester is weeks 1–13, second trimester is weeks 14–27, and third trimester runs from week 28 until birth.

Common questions

How far along am I really?▾
Pregnancy weeks are counted from the first day of your last period, not from conception. That means by the time you miss your period and get a positive pregnancy test, you're already considered 4 weeks pregnant. It feels weird at first, but it's the standard medical dating system used by all doctors and midwives.
What's the difference between gestational age and fetal age?▾
Gestational age is what doctors use — it counts from your last period. Fetal age counts from conception, which is about 2 weeks later. So when your doctor says you're 10 weeks pregnant, your baby is actually only 8 weeks old. The 2-week difference comes from the time before you even conceived.
Why does the week count keep changing?▾
Because pregnancy is measured in whole weeks, and your progress is continuous. You're always moving toward the next week — you're never exactly at one point. On Monday you might be 12 weeks 3 days, and by Friday you're 12 weeks 4 days. The calculator shows your current week and day count based on today's date.
When does the first trimester end?▾
The first trimester goes from week 1 to week 12. The second trimester is weeks 13 to 27, and the third trimester is weeks 28 to 40. These are just general divisions — not exact medical cutoffs. The risk of miscarriage drops significantly after the end of the first trimester, around 12 weeks.
