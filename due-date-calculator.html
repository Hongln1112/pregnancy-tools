  • Bookmark the page and recalculate anytime your care team updates your date.

How the due date is calculated

Due date = first day of last period + 280 days (40 weeks)

This is the standard method used by healthcare providers, called Naegele's Rule. It assumes a 28-day cycle with conception around day 14, then counts 40 weeks (280 days) forward. Your real due date may vary — only an ultrasound and your care team can pinpoint it more precisely.

Most pregnancies last between 37 and 42 weeks, so fewer than one in ten babies arrive exactly on their due date. Treat your estimate as a guide, not a promise — it simply tells you when to expect your little one and when the "full-term" window opens.

Common questions

How is my due date calculated?▾
The standard method adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last period. This is called Naegele's Rule and is the same formula most doctors use before an ultrasound.
Why is pregnancy counted from my last period, not conception?▾
Because the exact day of conception is rarely known, but the first day of your period is. Week 1 begins at your last period — about two weeks before conception typically occurs.
Is this calculator accurate?▾
It uses the standard 40-week guideline, but every pregnancy differs. Only an ultrasound and your care team can pinpoint your date precisely. Treat the result as a helpful estimate.
What if my due date changes later?▾
Due dates are often adjusted after the first ultrasound, especially if your cycle is irregular or you're unsure of your last period. Recalculate whenever your care team gives you a new date.
