How the due date is calculated

Due date = first day of last period + 280 days (40 weeks)

This is the standard method used by healthcare providers, called Naegele's Rule. It assumes a 28-day cycle with conception around day 14, then counts 40 weeks (280 days) forward. Your real due date may vary — only an ultrasound and your care team can pinpoint it more precisely.

Most pregnancies last between 37 and 42 weeks, so fewer than one in ten babies arrive exactly on their due date. Treat your estimate as a guide, not a promise — it simply tells you when to expect your little one and when the "full-term" window opens.

Common questions

How is my due date calculated?▾
The standard method adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period. This is called Naegele's Rule — subtract 3 months from your LMP, add 7 days, and add 1 year. Most doctors use this same formula before your first ultrasound confirms the dates. If your cycles are irregular, your ultrasound dating scan will be more accurate than the LMP method.
What if my cycles are irregular?▾
If your cycles aren't regular — say they're 35 days instead of 28 — your due date will be off. The LMP method assumes a 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14. If your cycles are longer, ovulation happens later, and your true due date is a few days later than the calculator says. Your first ultrasound will give you a more accurate date.
Is my due date exact?▾
No. Only about 5% of babies are born on their exact due date. A normal, healthy pregnancy lasts anywhere from 37 to 42 weeks. The due date is just an estimate — your doctor will monitor your baby's growth and your cervix at each checkup to decide if it's safe to wait or if induction is needed.
Should I use LMP or ultrasound dating?▾
Your dating ultrasound (usually done between 8 and 12 weeks) is more accurate than LMP, especially if your cycles are irregular. If the ultrasound date differs from your LMP due date by more than a week or so, your care provider will usually update your due date to match the ultrasound.
