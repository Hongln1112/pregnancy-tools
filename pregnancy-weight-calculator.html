Weight gain ranges by BMI

Based on guidelines from the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) for a single pregnancy:

  • Underweight (BMI under 18.5): gain 28–40 pounds
  • Normal weight (BMI 18.5–24.9): gain 25–35 pounds
  • Overweight (BMI 25–29.9): gain 15–25 pounds
  • Obese (BMI 30+): gain 11–20 pounds
These are totals for the whole pregnancy. Weight gain is usually slow in the first trimester and speeds up in the second and third.

Gaining within a healthy range supports your baby's growth and helps you recover more easily after birth. Every body is different — this range is a guideline, not a rule.

Common questions

How much weight should I gain during pregnancy?▾
It depends on your pre-pregnancy BMI. Underweight mothers gain 28–40 pounds, normal weight 25–35 pounds, overweight 15–25 pounds, and obese 11–20 pounds for a single pregnancy.
When does most weight gain happen?▾
Weight gain is usually slow in the first trimester and speeds up in the second and third trimesters. The ranges are totals for the whole pregnancy.
Do the guidelines differ for twins?▾
Yes. The ranges shown here are for single pregnancies. Weight gain guidelines are higher for twins or other multiples, so follow your doctor's specific advice.
