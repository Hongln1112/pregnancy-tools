The real signs labor is near

🩸
Bloody show
A pink or brown discharge — the mucus plug coming away. Can appear hours to days before active labor.
💧
Your water breaks
A gush or a steady trickle of fluid. This means labor is starting — call your care team, even if contractions aren't strong yet.
📉
Lightening (baby drops)
The baby settles lower in your pelvis. Breathing gets easier, but you'll need to urinate more.
⚡
Regular contractions that build
They become stronger, last longer, and come closer together over time — this is active labor.
🧹
Nesting energy
A sudden burst of energy to clean and prepare — a common but unreliable sign.

Go in right away if

Your water breaks — call even before contractions are strong.
You have heavy bleeding or bright-red discharge.
The baby moves much less than usual.
Contractions are very strong or you feel something isn't right — trust yourself.

Common questions

What are the first signs of labor starting?▾
Common early signs include your baby dropping lower (lightening), a release of the mucus plug called the bloody show, loose stools, a burst of nesting energy, and contractions that become stronger, longer, and closer together.
How do I tell real contractions from Braxton Hicks?▾
Real contractions get stronger, longer, and closer together, and walking makes them worse. Braxton Hicks are irregular, don't build in intensity, and often ease when you change position or drink water.
When should I go to the hospital?▾
Follow the 5-1-1 rule: contractions about every 5 minutes, lasting about 1 minute, for at least 1 hour. Also head in right away if your water breaks, you have heavy bleeding, or the baby's movements decrease.
Does labor start suddenly?▾
Sometimes. For first-time parents, labor often builds over hours or a day. It can also start suddenly with your water breaking or regular contractions. There's no single right way.
How long after the bloody show does labor start?▾
The bloody show can appear anywhere from a few hours to a few days before active labor. It's a sign labor is likely getting close, but not an exact timer.
