How to use this calculator

  • Enter how many weeks pregnant you are to see your baby's fruit size, length, and average weight for that week.
  • If you have an ultrasound weight estimate, add it in grams to see whether your baby is below, around, or above the weekly average.
  • Growth is steady and individual — check back each week and talk to your care team about any concerns.

About the estimates

Sizes and weights are general estimates for illustration

Every pregnancy grows a little differently, and ultrasound measurements are the most accurate way to check your baby's size and weight. The averages here are typical references — from around 300 g (10 oz) at week 20 to roughly 3,400 g (7.5 lb) at week 40 — but your baby's actual scan numbers are what matter most.

Common questions

How big is my baby at each week of pregnancy?▾
Sizes grow quickly — from a poppy seed around week 4 to a small pumpkin around week 40. This calculator gives a fruit comparison, length, and average weight for each week from 4 to 42.
How much should my baby weigh this week?▾
Weights rise steadily — around 300 g (10 oz) at week 20, about 1,000 g (2.2 lb) at week 28, and roughly 3,400 g (7.5 lb) at week 40. These are averages; every baby grows a little differently.
What does an ultrasound weight percentile mean?▾
A percentile compares your baby's estimated weight with other babies at the same week. Around the 50th percentile is average; below the 10th or above the 90th is worth discussing with your care team, but most are simply a sign of normal variation.
Are these fetal size estimates accurate?▾
They are general estimates for illustration. Every pregnancy grows a little differently, and ultrasound measurements are the most accurate way to check your baby's size and weight.
