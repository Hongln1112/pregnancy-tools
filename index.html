Common questions

Answers to the questions every expectant parent asks.

How is my due date calculated?▾
The standard method adds 280 days (40 weeks) to the first day of your last menstrual period. This is called Naegele's Rule — subtract 3 months from your LMP, add 7 days, and add 1 year. Most doctors use this same formula before your first ultrasound confirms the dates. If your cycles are irregular, your ultrasound dating scan will be more accurate than the LMP method.
Why is pregnancy counted from my last period, not conception?▾
Because the exact day of conception is rarely known — sperm can survive 3–5 days inside you, and the egg can be fertilized up to 24 hours after ovulation. The first day of your last period is a fixed, easy-to-remember date. Week 1 begins about two weeks before conception typically occurs, so by the time you know you're pregnant, you're already considered 4 weeks along.
Is my due date exact?▾
No. Only about 5% of babies are born on their exact due date. A normal, healthy pregnancy lasts anywhere from 37 to 42 weeks. The due date is just an estimate — your doctor or midwife will monitor your baby's growth, your cervix, and your overall health at each checkup to decide if induction is needed or if it's safe to wait a little longer.
How accurate is the fetal size estimate?▾
Our fetal size estimates are based on average measurements from standard growth charts. Every baby is different — some are naturally smaller, some larger. The charts show the typical size at each week, but your own baby's size on your ultrasound is what matters most. Ask your doctor if your baby's measurements are within the normal range for your gestational age.
Is my data stored anywhere?▾
No. All calculations run entirely in your browser — on your own device. Your last period date, weight, height, and any other numbers you enter never leave your computer or phone. We don't store them, don't transmit them, and don't use them for any kind of tracking or advertising targeting.
When should I call my doctor or midwife?▾
Contact your care provider right away if you experience heavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain, fluid leaking from the vagina, regular contractions before 37 weeks, decreased fetal movement after 28 weeks, or a fever over 100.4°F (38°C). If you're unsure whether something is normal, it's always better to call — that's what your midwife or doctor is there for.
Please note: These calculators and growth estimates are based on standard medical guidelines and are for informational purposes only. They are not a substitute for professional medical care. Always consult your doctor or midwife for advice specific to your pregnancy.
