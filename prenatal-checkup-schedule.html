    How to use this guide

    • Enter your current pregnancy week to see the typical appointments ahead of you.
    • Use it as a general map — the exact schedule, scans and screenings depend on your provider, country and personal history.
    • Always follow your own care team's plan; this guide is a helpful overview, not a prescription.

    A common appointment timeline

    Roughly monthly early on, weekly near the end
    • Weeks 8–12
      First prenatal visit — confirm pregnancy, date your due date, blood and urine tests, review your health history.
    • Weeks 11–13
      First-trimester screening — a blood test and an NT ultrasound to screen for common chromosome conditions.
    • Weeks 16–20
      Follow-up visits, and often a blood test for genetic screening (NIPT or maternal serum screen).
    • Weeks 18–22
      Anatomy scan — a detailed ultrasound checking your baby's structures; often reveals the sex.
    • Weeks 24–28
      Glucose tolerance test to screen for gestational diabetes, plus a routine visit.
    • Weeks 28–36
      Appointments every 2 weeks — growth checks, blood pressure, and your baby's position.
    • Weeks 36–40
      Weekly visits — final checks, birth planning, and monitoring until delivery.

    Common questions

    How often are prenatal appointments?▾
    A common schedule is about once a month in the first two trimesters, every 2 weeks around weeks 28–36, then weekly from week 36 to delivery. Exact timing varies by provider.
    What is the first-trimester screening?▾
    Around 11–13 weeks you may have a combined screening, including a blood test and an ultrasound to measure the nuchal translucency (NT). This screens for common chromosome conditions.
    When is the anatomy scan?▾
    The detailed anatomy scan (also called the 20-week scan) usually happens around 18–22 weeks. It checks your baby's structures and can often reveal the sex.
    When is the glucose tolerance test?▾
    Gestational diabetes screening is commonly done around 24–28 weeks with a glucose tolerance test, though timing can vary.
