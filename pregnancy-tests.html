Pregnancy Tests & Screenings

A plain-language look at the common prenatal checks — what each one does, when it happens, and what to make of your results.

Related: Checkup Schedule · Symptoms Guide

First prenatal blood work

Weeks 8–12
Confirms your pregnancy, checks your blood type and Rh factor, and screens for anaemia and a few infections. It also establishes a baseline for your whole pregnancy.
Your care team uses this to plan follow-ups — for example, whether you need an Rh antibody check.

First-trimester screening (NT)

Weeks 11–13
A blood test plus an ultrasound that measures the nuchal translucency behind your baby's neck. Together they estimate the chance of common chromosome conditions.
It's a screening, not a diagnosis — it only estimates risk. If the result is elevated, you'll be offered further, more detailed testing.

Genetic screening (NIPT or serum test)

Weeks 16–20
NIPT looks at DNA from the placenta in your blood to estimate the chance of conditions like Down syndrome. A maternal serum screen is an alternative blood option.
These are risk estimates too. An atypical result leads to a diagnostic test before any conclusions.

Anatomy (20-week) scan

Weeks 18–22
A detailed ultrasound that checks your baby's heart, brain, spine, organs, limbs and growth. It can often reveal the sex if you'd like to know.
It's a routine structural check. Most scans find no concerns — this is one of the most reassuring visits of your pregnancy.

Glucose tolerance test

Weeks 24–28
You drink a sugary drink and your blood sugar is checked to screen for gestational diabetes.
Found early, gestational diabetes is usually managed well with diet and monitoring, protecting both you and your baby.

Group B strep swab

Weeks 36–37
A quick swab checks whether you carry Group B streptococcus bacteria. It's harmless to you but can affect a newborn during delivery.
If positive, antibiotics during labor greatly reduce the risk to your baby. Simple and routine.

Common questions

What is the first-trimester screening?▾
Around 11–13 weeks, a blood test and an NT ultrasound screen for common chromosome conditions. It estimates risk — a 'screen', not a diagnosis.
What is the anatomy (20-week) scan?▾
Around 18–22 weeks, a detailed ultrasound checks your baby's organs and structures, and often reveals the sex. It's a routine structural check.
Is the glucose tolerance test necessary?▾
Screening for gestational diabetes is commonly offered around 24–28 weeks. Early detection lets you manage blood sugar to protect you and your baby.
If a screening result is 'positive', am I diagnosed?▾
No. A screening only estimates risk. A 'screen positive' simply means further, more accurate diagnostic testing is offered to confirm or rule out a condition.
