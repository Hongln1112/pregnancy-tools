Birth Preparation Checklist

Pack calmly, recognize true labor, and know when to go. This checklist keeps the essentials in one place for when it counts.

Related: Checkup Schedule · Pregnancy Week

Hospital bag essentials Have it ready by week 36

For you

  • Documents & ID, insurance and birth plan.
  • Comfortable, loose clothing and slippers.
  • Chargers, phone, and a small speaker if you like.
  • Toiletries, hair ties and basic cosmetics.
  • Snacks and a refillable water bottle.
  • Pillow and light robe for comfort.

For baby

  • A going-home outfit and a soft blanket.
  • A few newborn nappies and wipes.
  • An infant car seat, already installed.
  • Mittens and a hat for the early days.

Signs that labor may be starting Learn these early

True labor usually brings regular contractions that get longer, stronger and closer together. Other signs include your water breaking or a small "show" of blood-streaked mucus.

True labor

  • Contractions at a steady, increasing rhythm.
  • Pain that builds and doesn't ease with rest.
  • Walking may actually strengthen them.

False labor (Braxton Hicks)

  • Irregular contractions that don't get closer.
  • Often eases with movement or changing position.
  • No progressive pattern — more of an ache.

Call your care team or go in if

  • Your water breaks (even without contractions).
  • Contractions are regular — commonly about 4–5 minutes apart, lasting a minute, for an hour.
  • You notice reduced baby movement.
  • You have heavy bleeding or severe, continuous pain.

Common questions

When should I pack my hospital bag?▾
Most care teams suggest having it ready by around 36 weeks, since labor can begin any time after that.
What are the signs of true labor?▾
Regular contractions that get longer, stronger and closer together, water breaking, or a bloody 'show' can all signal labor. Call your care team when you suspect it.
When should I go to the hospital?▾
Follow your care team's guidance — commonly when contractions are regular and about 4–5 minutes apart lasting a minute for an hour, or when your water breaks. Always call first.
